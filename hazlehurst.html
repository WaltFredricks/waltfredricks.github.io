Executive Summary

This updated plan refines the rapid renovation of neighborhoods in Hazlehurst, Georgia, across the railroad tracks from the community center, incorporating a military-style "combat engineering" approach with a detailed Gantt chart for a 14-day timeline. The project targets 150–200 older homes, 5–10 derelict buildings, and one abandoned factory (10,000–20,000 sq ft), with all roofs specified as metal. The area is divided into five zones for parallel operations, ensuring no crew conflicts. The workforce remains ~300–350 skilled workers, leveraging an unlimited labor pool for maximum concurrency.

Zone Breakdown

  • Zone A: North side of tracks, near Community Center (~50 homes).
  • Zone B: Mid-neighborhood blocks behind Zone A (~50–75 homes).
  • Zone C: Far west neighborhood near ballfields (~50–75 homes).
  • Zone D: Derelict buildings and street-front structures (5–10 sites).
  • Zone E: Abandoned factory and adjacent industrial lots (1 site).

Logistics and Resources

Materials

  • Metal Roofing: Homes: 200 × 25 squares/home = 5,000 squares (~$1.5M total installed).
  • Derelicts: 10 × 25 squares = 250 squares (~$75,000).
  • Factory: 20,000 sq ft ÷ 100 = 200 squares (~$60,000).
  • Total Metal Roofing: ~5,450 squares, ~$1.635M (material + labor).
  • Paint: 200 homes × 20 gal/home = 4,000 gal (~$120,000 at $30/gal).
  • Landscaping: Sod, shrubs, mulch for 200 homes + Zones D/E (~$200,000).
  • Structural: Lumber, concrete, steel for Zones D/E (~$250,000).
  • Total Materials: ~$2.1M.

Other Resources

  • Equipment: Rent 50 vehicles (excavators, lifts, trucks).
  • Worker Support: Temporary housing, meals, PPE for 350 workers.
  • Coordination: Project software, daily briefings.

Rough Cost Estimate

  • Labor: ~$50/hr × 350 workers × 140 hrs = ~$2.45M
  • Materials: ~$2.1M
  • Total: ~$4.6–5.5M (excluding overhead)

Potential Challenges and Mitigations

  • Railroad Proximity: Coordinate with Norfolk Southern for safety barriers.
  • Weather: August heat and rain; provide hydration stations and flexible indoor tasks.
  • Community Impact: Notify residents in advance; stage work to minimize disruption.
  • Regulatory: Secure permits and verify derelict ownership beforehand.
  • Metal Roofing: Requires skilled roofers; pre-order materials to avoid delays.

Conclusion

This plan integrates a zone-based, wave-driven approach with metal roofing and a precise Gantt chart, ensuring a 14-day completion with approximately 300–350 workers. It provides a scalable model for rapid urban renewal.
